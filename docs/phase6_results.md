# Phase 6 results

Generated by `python -m ayurveda_kg.phase6`. Do not edit by hand.

**What this is.** A GraphRAG assistant over two public-domain classical translations (Kaviratna *Charaka-Samhita*, Bhishagratna *Sushruta Samhita*) that retrieves passages, text-graph facts and project knowledge-graph facts (herb-drug research risk scores), answers with checkable citations using a local model (`qwen3:8b`), and always appends a research-use disclaimer. AyurParam is not used yet (deferred).

## Corpus and graph

- 6493 passages (Charaka: 3912, Sushruta: 2581).
- The alias lexicon finds 16 of 20 scoped herbs in the texts.
- Text graph: 211 co-occurrence edges (min 2 passages); LLM triple extraction over 476 herb+condition passages accepted 532 grounded triples and rejected 710 (0 model errors). Relations: treats 434, pacifies 88, aggravates 10.
- A triple is accepted only if its subject and object literally appear in the passage and resolve to lexicon entities. This removes hallucinated entities; the **precision of accepted triples is unverified until an expert checks a sample**.

## Over-reading of co-occurrence facts

After the first run, reading the answers showed the model turning 'X is co-mentioned with Y' into 'X balances Y'. The graph-fact wording and the prompt rules were then revised (this was done **after** seeing those outputs, so it is a fix, not a blind test). The same herb-condition questions were answered with the old and the new wording/rules; a sentence counts as an over-reading when it cites only co-occurrence facts but states a therapeutic role (automatic keyword check, a lower bound).

- before the fix: 1 of 16 sentences that cite only co-occurrence facts state a therapeutic role (6%) across 30 questions.
- after the fix: 0 of 17 sentences that cite only co-occurrence facts state a therapeutic role (0%) across 30 questions.

Correctness: recall of the gold drugs/herbs (top-drugs and top-herbs questions) or whether the gold score appears within 0.03 (score questions). Citation validity: fraction of cited ids that exist in the retrieved context. Support rate: fraction of answer sentences whose content words appear in the cited sources (a lexical proxy, not an entailment judgement).

## Limitations

- **No expert-verified question set yet.** A CSV template (`data/processed/rag/expert_template.csv`) is ready; until an Ayurveda expert fills and verifies it, no claim about answer correctness on real questions is made.
- Faithfulness and hallucination numbers are automatic proxies (citation validity, lexical support). They catch invented citations and unsupported sentences, not subtle errors.
- **A known, undetected failure mode:** an irrelevant retrieved passage can be blended into an answer. In the ginger example the answer states that ginger is relevant to nausea and vomiting by citing a passage that is actually about *Randia dumetorum*. The lexical-support proxy scores this as fully supported, so only reading the answers (or an expert) catches it.
- The prompt rules were revised after reading the first run's outputs (see the over-reading section); the K-first prompt variant was also designed after seeing failures. The question sets are small (12 / 20 / 35 / 60 questions) and the model is a single 8B local LLM, so differences of a few answers are within noise.
- The texts are OCR output: some passages are noisy, there are no page numbers (citations use passage ids), and front-matter (testimonials) can be retrieved.
- Triple extraction precision is unverified; object linking is by containment and can over-link (for example a phrase containing the word 'wind' links to the vata dosha).
- Only two English translations are used; classical terminology varies, and the alias lexicon is a hand-written v1 for an advisor to review.
- Generation uses a general-purpose open LLM (`qwen3:8b`). The AyurParam comparison from the plan is deferred (the project owner has not approved the download).
- Interaction facts are research risk scores (mechanistic hypotheses with known false alarms), never advice.
